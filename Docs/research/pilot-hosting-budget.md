# Encrypted-library hosting inside the personal-pilot budget

Research date: 23 October 2026. Research for [Research encrypted-library hosting within the US$10 personal-pilot budget](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/20), informing [Choose Android and backend boundaries for encrypted offline libraries](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/8). Prices are published US-dollar baseline rates, without promotional credits. This note selects no vendor, technology stack, transfer policy, or quota-accounting rule.

## Finding

The modeled pilot fits beneath US$10 before tax on Cloudflare Workers Paid + D1 + R2 (approximately **$5.45/month**) or a DigitalOcean Basic VM with daily disk backups (**$7.80/month**). These are workload estimates, not enforceable total-bill ceilings. Both expose metered overages; neither provider's budget alert stops the relevant usage. Supabase's complete managed bundle fails the constraint: Free has insufficient file storage and Pro starts at $25. A free database combined with external object storage is another possible shape, with additional administration and availability dependencies. Evidence and assumptions follow.

No examined, fully verified candidate establishes an unconditional **$10 final invoice maximum**, including abuse, taxes, and payment conversion. The architecture decision must retain this gap instead of converting a low expected bill into a spending guarantee. A fixed-price, unmetered offering with verified renewal, backup, tax, and retention terms could change that finding; a headline price alone cannot.

## Binding product contract and evaluation workload

The [confirmed planning inputs](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/8#issuecomment-6366302786) are one person testing one Android device, a firm 5 GB cloud upload/storage limit per account, a $10 total monthly ceiling, solo maintenance, and no mandatory stack/provider. Product multi-device behavior remains required despite the smaller pilot.

The [privacy resolution](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/5#issuecomment-6039656388) requires Android encryption of EPUBs and descriptive metadata, operational metadata only on the backend, no backend library keys/root Recovery code/provider credentials, atomic server-time account decisions, active deletion, backup expiration within 30 days after permanent deletion, request/security logs no older than 7 days, and only an opaque account identifier plus deletion time retained for at most 60 days. Restoring backups must not reactivate deleted accounts or expose their data. Read this together with the [library contract](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/12) and [Settings resolution](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/15#issuecomment-6076966797): first service-accepted same-field change wins over a stale edit; removals/Collection deletion have their agreed precedence; uncertain operations require reconciliation; old queues cannot attach to a replacement account.

The following are costing assumptions, not new product rules:

| Input | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Units | 5 GB = 5,000,000,000 ciphertext bytes for this calculation. Whether the user limit measures original files, ciphertext, covers/metadata, or reservations remains undecided. 5 GiB would be about 7.4% larger. |
| Library shape | 1,000 objects averaging 5 MB, with smaller encrypted metadata and a 0.1 GB operational database. Object count is assumed rather than measured. |
| Monthly writes | One complete 5 GB upload/replacement during the month. No unbounded retained generations. |
| Light transfer | One complete download: 5 GB/month to the device. |
| Repeated transfer | Thirty complete downloads: 150 GB/month to the device. This is a stress scenario, not permission to impose a download allowance. |
| API/metadata work | 100,000 API invocations at assumed average 10 ms CPU, 10 million relational rows read, 100,000 written, and at most 200,000 log events/month, including maintenance. These must be measured; cryptographic authentication cost depends on the [security-protocol decision](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/13). |
| Object backup model | Seven retained daily full 5 GB ciphertext snapshots: 35 GB, plus 5 GB live. Thirty snapshot runs/month mean approximately 30,000 object writes and 30,000 reads, plus listings/manifests. No compression savings assumed. |
| Temporary headroom | Baseline 40 GB object retention excludes upload staging, expired-but-not-yet-purged objects, metadata copies, and a snapshot in progress. A separate 10 GB sustained allowance is shown below. |
| Dictionary delivery | Actual application pack sizes are unknown; use total hosted pack size **D GB** and monthly pack downloads **N**. Source archives are not measured application packs. |

Neither 5 GB/account nor one pilot account bounds backup history, abandoned uploads, repeated downloads, unauthenticated requests, log volume, or newly created accounts. Those are distinct cost inputs.

## Provider evidence and shortlist

### Managed: Cloudflare Workers + D1 + R2 Standard

Workers Paid has a **$5/account/month minimum**. It includes **10 million requests** and **30 million CPU ms**; excess costs **$0.30/million requests** and **$0.02/million CPU ms**. Workers has no bandwidth charge. Free instead allows **100,000 requests/day and 10 ms CPU/invocation**. CPU caps are per invocation, not a monthly invoice ceiling. These figures concern Workers, not Containers. [Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/).

D1 holds relational operational state, not EPUB blobs. Paid includes **25 billion rows read/month, 50 million written/month, and 5 GB**; overages are **$0.001/million read, $1/million written, $0.75/GB-month**. Free includes 5 million reads/day, 100,000 writes/day, and 5 GB total; reaching a free allowance blocks queries/storage growth, while paid excess is billed. There is no capacity-hour charge while idle. [D1 pricing and limit behavior](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/).

R2 Standard includes **10 GB-month, 1 million Class A operations, and 10 million Class B operations/month**. Beyond these: **$0.015/GB-month**, **$4.50/million A**, **$0.36/million B**; direct Internet egress is free. Billing rounds to whole GB-month/million-operation units. Standard is used here; Infrequent Access has retrieval fees and minimum storage duration. Other attached services can bill separately. [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/).

Workers Logs Paid currently retains logs **7 days**, includes **20 million events/month**, then **$0.60/million**. Free retains 3 days. **A pricing change is announced for 1 December 2026**: recalculate before deployment crossing that date. Configure sampling/redaction or disable persistence; default invocation logging can contain request details. This evidence describes this logging product, not every provider security record. [Workers Logs limits and pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/).

There is no inactivity-week pause in these documented Worker/D1 execution models. Free is a plausible experiment, subject to the CPU limit and actual security implementation; it does not make R2 a hard-capped account. Authentication feasibility, rate-limiting costs, and practical Android upload behavior remain unmeasured. Worker memory is 128 MB; a Free-zone request body limit is 100 MB independently of buying Workers Paid. Streaming or multipart upload is therefore relevant; do not hold an entire EPUB in Worker memory. Static asset files are limited to 25 MiB each. [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/).

### Simple self-hosted: one API + SQLite + private files on DigitalOcean

A Basic Regular VM with **1 GiB RAM, 25 GiB SSD, one vCPU and 1,000 GiB monthly outbound allowance costs $6/month**. The $4 tier has only 512 MiB RAM/10 GiB disk; 5 GB live data plus operating system, database, replacement staging and safe free space make it a poor planning baseline. Billing is per second from 2026, with a minimum 60 seconds or $0.01, whichever is higher; a stopped-but-retained VM is still a provisioned resource. [Droplet pricing](https://www.digitalocean.com/pricing/droplets).

Basic weekly backup adds **20% ($1.20)**; daily adds **30% ($1.80)**. Use the fixed-percentage Basic backup plan in this estimate, not the separately priced usage-based plan. [Backup pricing](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/backups/details/pricing/). Weekly images are retained **four weeks**; daily images **seven days**. Converted snapshots survive until deleted, so conversion would require additional explicit retention/cost handling. [Backup retention](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/backups/getting-started/quickstart/).

These backups cover the disk, hence the full 5 GB library if it resides there; they are crash-consistent and stored in the same data center. They do not establish application consistency or cross-provider disaster recovery. A restore drill must check database/file agreement. [Backup features](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/backups/details/features/). The solo maintainer owns operating-system patches, TLS, process supervision, database maintenance, storage monitoring, backup checks and log configuration. That operational burden is an architectural inference, not a measured monthly labor estimate.

Inbound transfer is free; excess outbound is **$0.01/GiB**. Allowance accrues over the VM's lifetime within the month; public-interface transfers, including remote object backups, count. A fresh late-month VM must not be costed as having a full month's allowance. [Bandwidth billing](https://docs.digitalocean.com/platform/billing/bandwidth/).

### Managed comparison: Supabase

Free is **$0**, with **500 MB database, 1 GB file storage, 5 GB uncached and 5 GB cached egress**; projects pause after one inactive week. It cannot hold this 5 GB library using Supabase Storage. Pro starts at **$25/month**, including the first project's compute through the recurring $10 compute credit, so it already exceeds the ceiling. Pro includes seven-day daily database backups/log retention. [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing).

Supabase database backups explicitly **exclude Storage objects**; a library-object backup must be costed independently. Free projects should export databases themselves. [Backup coverage](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups). A Free Supabase database/API with R2 blobs might remain inexpensive, but introduces a second provider, project-pausing recovery and independent backup/retention work. It is not evaluated as a fully verified hard-ceiling solution, and built-in authentication must not silently override the accepted account/recovery policy.

### Optional independent ciphertext backup destination

Backblaze B2 currently advertises **$6.95/TB-month**, first **10 GB free**, no minimum file/storage duration, free Class A/B/C transactions, free egress up to three times average stored data and then **$0.01/GB**. Class D operations are separately metered. A separate 35 GB backup set is approximately **$0.174/month** above the free 10 GB, before transfer/tax. This uses decimal units and published baseline arithmetic, not a bill quote. A provider-independent copy is optional here, not a new requirement. [B2 pricing](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing).

B2 has configurable **daily** cost caps for storage/download/transaction categories, but excludes Class D (such as Event Notification outbound calls). Caps on a backup destination do not cap API compute or VM egress, and a daily amount must be budgeted over a 31-day month. An exhausted cap can prevent new backups; existing-data retention must still meet deletion rules. [B2 caps and alerts](https://www.backblaze.com/docs/en/cloud-storage-data-caps-and-alerts).

### Fixed-price lead needing a verified order quote

The [OVHcloud US VPS page](https://us.ovhcloud.com/vps/) advertises VPS-1 from **$4.54/month**, with 2 vCores, 4 GB RAM, 40 GB NVMe, daily previous-day backup and unlimited traffic at 500 Mbps. The advertised Configure link selects **12-month upfront pricing**, so $4.54 × 12 = **$54.48 before tax** cannot be treated as a $4.54 monthly cash invoice. The no-commitment monthly price, renewal, region and final tax were not verified. Its [options page](https://us.ovhcloud.com/vps/options/) describes an included 24-hour backup and an optional seven-day backup from $1.40/month. This is a lead for a bounded bundle quote if needed, not a verified budget candidate; retention/logging and restore requirements still apply.

## Monthly scenario arithmetic

Calculated from the assumptions above and cited unit rates; these are original planning calculations, not provider promises. Included allowances are assumed otherwise unused by other projects in the same account.

| Shape | Light: 5 GB device downloads | Repeated: 150 GB device downloads | What can change the result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Workers Paid + D1 + R2 | **$5.45** | **$5.45** | $5 compute + (40−10) × $0.015 storage. Modeled requests, rows and logs fit included amounts; R2 has no per-GB egress fee. |
| Same, with 10 GB additional sustained staging/snapshot headroom | **$5.60** | **$5.60** | (50−10) × $0.015 + $5. Extra object operations must still fit assumptions. |
| Workers Free + D1 + R2 | **$0.45** | **$0.45** | Same storage arithmetic, but daily limits/10 ms execution can reject requests; authentication cost unverified. |
| $6 VM + daily Basic backups | **$7.80** | **$7.80** | Full-month VM, $1.80 backup, modeled outbound below allowance. Database/files fit the disk only with monitoring and bounded staging. |
| $6 VM + weekly Basic backups | **$7.20** | **$7.20** | Four-week recovery history; up to a week of lost changes. Backup logs/records need special treatment below. |
| Supabase alone | **Not suitable** | **Not suitable** | Free storage insufficient; Pro $25 minimum before object-backup changes. |

The R2 operation assumption is about 31,000 Class A writes plus listings, and 31,000 reads for light / 60,000 for repeated downloads including backup reads, comfortably inside its allowances. Multipart chunks, retries, covers, manifests, ranges and abuse can increase request counts. Seven full retained snapshots are deliberately conservative for stable ciphertext; content-addressed/incremental backups could reduce storage but require separate design/restore evidence.

For an independent B2 backup instead of same-provider object copies, charge its 35 GB separately and remove those 35 GB from the source-provider backup storage assumption; do not double-count both unless retaining both sets. Thirty full source exports produce **150 GB/month additional outbound** on a VM. With repeated downloads that is 300 GB decimal (about 279.4 GiB), still below a full-month 1,000 GiB allowance before protocol/API/dictionary traffic. The VM + daily disk images + B2 copies would instead be about **$7.974**, retaining both forms of backup.

Dictionary packs add **D GB stored** and **N × D GB delivered/month**, plus request/chunk counts. On R2, add stored bytes to the shared storage allowance and Class B operations; direct delivery adds no per-GB egress charge. On the VM, include delivery in the outbound allowance and disk capacity. For example, a purely hypothetical 0.5 GB pack downloaded 30 times adds 15 GB outbound; it is **not** evidence of an actual pack size. Workers Static Assets may work only if actual files meet the 25 MiB limit (or use a separately designed split format); R2 avoids assuming that limit is met. Dictionary lookup remains local and sends no selected words/passages, as required by [Define](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/14#issuecomment-6077225498).

## Ceiling enforcement, currency and headroom

Cloudflare [budget alerts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/billing/manage/budget-alerts/) explicitly notify without pausing/capping usage. DigitalOcean [spend alerts](https://docs.digitalocean.com/platform/billing/spend-alerts/) are not an overall spend cap; its special Serverless Inference key pause does not apply to a library VM. Supabase's [Spend Cap](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/cost-control) is Pro-only, covers selected usage items and excludes compute and various add-ons; it cannot reduce the $25 subscription to $10.

A relational byte reservation can prevent concurrent uploads exceeding a chosen account quota. It cannot stop billable unauthenticated Worker requests, SQL scans, logs, public dictionary requests or VM transfer overages. Application counters, rate limits and manual shutdown lower risk; without a provider-enforced aggregate limit and verified billing semantics they do not prove the invoice ceiling. Tests must include retries, abandoned multipart uploads and pre-authentication traffic, not just the happy-path 5 GB upload.

Published USD totals leave **$4.55** headroom at $5.45, or **$2.20** at $7.80, before tax/payment costs. Cloudflare determines applicable tax from billing details; the user's timezone is not proof of their billing address. [Cloudflare sales tax](https://developers.cloudflare.com/billing/understand/sales-tax/). DigitalOcean issues USD invoices and applies location-dependent tax. [DigitalOcean tax/billing overview](https://docs.digitalocean.com/platform/billing/taxes/).

For sensitivity only, 15% tax makes $5.45 about **$6.27** and $7.80 **$8.97**, leaving about $3.73/$1.03 before card/FX charges. Actual tax must be verified at order time: DigitalOcean's [South Africa page](https://docs.digitalocean.com/platform/billing/taxes/zaf/) currently displays 15.5%, whereas [SARS's current rate page](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/other-taxes/) states 15%. That documentation discrepancy prevents presenting a final South African invoice quote. At 15.5%, the VM example would be about $9.01. No live currency conversion is needed because the ceiling itself is USD; the payment provider's exchange rate/fees are unknown.

The baseline includes API compute, relational state, 5 GB live ciphertext, stated backups, modeled transfers and logs within included amounts. It does **not** silently authorize paid domains, email/SMS, managed database add-ons, paid monitoring, extra accounts, malware-scanning SaaS, or additional restore VMs. If needed for the implementation, those must fit the same ceiling or reopen feasibility. Maintainer labor, device mobile data and app-store fees are outside this hosting/transfer/backup comparison and are not claimed free.

## Retention and restore feasibility

| Contract | Cloudflare shape | VM shape / limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active deletion and authority revocation | R2 deletes are strongly consistent through direct APIs; cached custom-domain objects can survive until cache expiry/purge. Private content needs live account authorization and controlled caching. | Revoke authority atomically before cleanup; remove files/rows and retry failed deletion. Disk unlink does not prove provider forensic erasure. |
| Ciphertext backups gone within 30 days of permanent deletion | D1 Paid Time Travel is always on with a 30-day history (Free 7 days); this is database history, not an R2 backup. Seven-day object snapshots can fit, with explicit purge and verification. R2 lifecycle deletion is typically within 24h and can take longer, so lifecycle at day 30 alone is insufficient proof. | Daily 7-day / weekly 4-week images fit a modeled <=30-day window. Never retain unbounded converted snapshots. Confirm deletion/expiry actually happens and check separate object backup versions. |
| Request/security logs <=7 days | Workers Logs Paid 7 days matches this product's advertised retention. Never copy logs into 30-day D1 history or library backups. Broader provider logs remain unverified. | Rotate/remove active logs early enough for **all copies**. Seven-day active logs in seven-day images can survive ~14 days; weekly images can extend this to ~35 days. Keep persisted request/security logs outside image-backed data or avoid persistence; do not claim compliance from active log rotation alone. |
| Opaque deletion record <=60 days | Deleting a record after 60 active days can leave it in Time Travel another 30. Use a design accounting for backup/history lifetime, e.g. earlier active expiry or a separate store, without losing restore protection. | Disk/object backups also extend record lifetime. Budget the combined active-plus-backup horizon, or exclude these records from snapshots and maintain an independent bounded restore exclusion ledger. |
| Restore must not resurrect accounts/data | Restore into an unavailable/quarantined service; reconcile a current deletion exclusion ledger and deadlines before serving. Restoring D1 does not erase older restore bookmarks. | Restore images while cloud traffic is blocked, reconcile current deletion state and scrub deleted account files/rows before exposing the API. A second billable restore machine must be costed if required. |

Sources: [R2 consistency and cache caveats](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/), [R2 object lifecycle timing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/), [D1 Time Travel and restore behavior](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/reference/time-travel/), and the backup/log sources above. These are operational capability findings, not claims of instantaneous global physical erasure. Cloudflare's advertised R2 durability is not a user-controlled backup against authorized accidental deletion. Same-account backup buckets and same-data-center VM images also share failure/credential risks.

Provider-held infrastructure, fraud/security, billing and control-plane records have not been shown to obey the application's seven-day request/security limit or sixty-day deletion-record limit. A provider DPA/privacy policy does not automatically establish per-product retention. Before adoption, identify records in scope, configurable retention, service-controlled backup erasure, support visibility and provider obligations. Do not silently reinterpret the accepted policy to exempt inconvenient records. For reference, [DigitalOcean's privacy policy](https://www.digitalocean.com/legal/privacy-policy) has broader retention purposes and is not a specific seven-day tenant-request log guarantee.

## Consequences for the Android/backend boundary

D1's [`batch()`](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/worker-api/d1-database/) executes a prepared-statement group transactionally and rolls back the sequence on failure. SQLite supports only one simultaneous writer and offers explicit transactions such as `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`; its [transaction documentation](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_transaction.html) supplies the corresponding VM primitive. These establish possible building blocks, not a proof of a correct Cloud EPUB implementation.

The resulting design constraints are inferences from those primitives and the accepted contracts:

1. **Relational authority.** Store opaque account/device/session identifiers, state/deadlines, operational byte counts/reservations, object references, service revisions and operation outcomes together. Keep descriptive fields and search on the encrypted client side. Encrypted blobs need version/operation structure that can enforce agreed field-level conflict and membership semantics without plaintext names or dates; choose the representation through the [Android/backend boundary decision](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/8) and [security-protocol decision](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/13).
2. **One acceptance boundary.** Validate account authority, server-time deadline, expected field revision, reservation balance and idempotency identity in the same atomic acceptance decision. A read followed by an unguarded write is insufficient. A conditional update affecting zero rows must cause the intended rejection/rollback, not silently allow later batch writes. Test competing same-field edits, simultaneous quota reservations and activity racing deletion.
3. **Objects are a separate commit domain.** A database transaction cannot atomically include a filesystem/R2 upload. Use a designed reserve/stage/finalize/reconcile sequence, immutable object identities or guarded writes, limits for actual bytes and bounded abandoned-upload cleanup. Avoid making uncommitted ciphertext visible or releasing reservations before cleanup behavior is known. This is not a chosen protocol.
4. **Retry outcomes.** Persist operation identity and accepted result sufficiently to reconcile a lost response without repeating password/recovery/quota mutations. R2's strong read consistency does not mean a 404 proves an earlier in-flight upload failed; it can commit later. [R2 in-flight-write caveat](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/). Operation records need retention classification; do not keep account-identifying outcomes forever under the name of idempotency.
5. **Expiry and queue isolation.** Delayed workers recheck state/version; successful earlier qualifying activity invalidates stale deletion work. Restoration must finish strictly before the server deadline. The client binds every queue and stored credential to the opaque account identity and local authorization generation, clears them under Sign out policy, and never replays them into a fresh registration sharing a username. Hosting supplies no such behavior automatically.
6. **Scheduled work and evidence.** Expiry, backup pruning, abandoned-upload cleanup and deletion-record expiry require reliable scheduled maintenance with retries/monitoring inside the cost assumptions. A suspended API does not suspend retention obligations. Verify status freshness and unknown-result reconciliation required by Settings, then run a restore drill with a deleted account, expired account, live account and delayed upload before adoption.

## Decision handoff

Evidence favors taking the Cloudflare paid shape and the single-VM shape into the architecture discussion, with managed maintenance versus VM operational control as a real tradeoff. Supabase all-in-one paid hosting is outside the current ceiling. This is a shortlist for evaluation, not vendor adoption.

Still unresolved in the [Android/backend boundary decision](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/8): quota units/accounting; treatment of metadata, encryption overhead and in-flight reservations; full-quota/offline behavior; replacement/deletion reclamation; number of allowed accounts; transfer/abuse controls; dictionary pack measurement; baseline backup recovery point/object-history policy; and the concrete spending-control approach. The [security-protocol decision](https://github.com/A3R0-01/Cloud-Epub/issues/13) owns authentication/encryption authority and its measured resource needs. Provider-level retention and an enforceable final-bill ceiling remain explicit feasibility gaps. No cloud resources were provisioned, secrets supplied, stack implemented, or PR opened by this research.
